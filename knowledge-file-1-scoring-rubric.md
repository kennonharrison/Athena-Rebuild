# Knowledge File 1: Scoring Rubric

## How Scoring Works

Each of the 24 subskills is scored on a 0-100 scale. Scores are derived from two sources: resume analysis (Path A) and conversational responses (both paths).

Scoring is relative to a general working adult population. A score of 50 = average. 70+ = notably strong. 85+ = exceptional. Below 40 = area for significant development.

---

## RESUME EXTRACTION RUBRIC

When a user uploads a resume, score the following subskills based on observable evidence:

### Directly Scorable from Resume (typically 14-18 subskills)

**Initiative (0-100)**
- 80-100: Founded a business, launched programs, created roles that didn't exist, led projects without being asked
- 60-79: Took on additional responsibilities, led projects, sought promotions
- 40-59: Standard progression, responsibilities aligned with role expectations
- 20-39: Very linear, passive career trajectory
- Evidence: Career progression speed, self-started projects, entrepreneurial activity, volunteer leadership

**Self-discipline (0-100)**
- 80-100: Long tenures, advanced degrees completed while working, certifications requiring sustained effort
- 60-79: Consistent employment, completed degree, professional development visible
- 40-59: Average tenure patterns, standard education completion
- 20-39: Very short tenures, incomplete education patterns
- Evidence: Job tenure, educational completion, certification accumulation

**Responsibility (0-100)**
- 80-100: P&L ownership, team management, client-facing accountability, scope increased over time
- 60-79: Managed projects or small teams, clear ownership language in descriptions
- 40-59: Individual contributor with defined responsibilities
- 20-39: Limited scope, minimal ownership language
- Evidence: Management scope, budget responsibility, accountability language ("led," "owned," "managed" vs. "helped," "assisted," "supported")

**Organization (0-100)**
- 80-100: Resume itself is exceptionally well-structured; roles show project management, process improvement, systems thinking
- 60-79: Well-organized resume; roles include planning/coordination
- 40-59: Adequate organization; standard role descriptions
- 20-39: Disorganized resume; scattered career narrative
- Evidence: Resume structure quality, project management experience, process/systems language

**Language Use (0-100)**
- 80-100: Precise, varied vocabulary; strong action verbs; error-free; sophisticated phrasing
- 60-79: Clear writing; good grammar; appropriate vocabulary
- 40-59: Functional but unremarkable writing
- 20-39: Errors, vague language, unclear descriptions
- Evidence: Writing quality of resume itself

**Formality (0-100)**
- 80-100: Professional tone perfectly calibrated; industry-appropriate language
- 60-79: Appropriately professional
- 40-59: Functional but unpolished
- 20-39: Too casual or inconsistent register
- Evidence: Resume tone and formatting

**Verbal Communication (partial, 0-100)**
- Can infer from: presentations mentioned, client-facing roles, teaching/training, public speaking
- 80-100: Frequent presentations, spokesperson roles, teaching, media appearances
- 60-79: Client-facing, team presentations, some public speaking
- 40-59: Standard communication requirements
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 75 from resume alone

**Collaboration (partial, 0-100)**
- Can infer from: cross-functional projects, team size managed, partnership language
- 80-100: Led cross-functional teams, multi-stakeholder projects, partnership development
- 60-79: Regular team-based work, collaboration mentioned
- 40-59: Standard teamwork
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 70 from resume alone

**Curiosity (0-100)**
- 80-100: Multiple fields/industries, continuous learning visible, diverse certifications, career pivots showing exploration
- 60-79: Some professional development, industry breadth
- 40-59: Single-track career, minimal visible learning
- 20-39: No evidence of exploration or development
- Evidence: Career diversity, education beyond requirements, certifications, industry transitions

**Creativity (partial, 0-100)**
- 80-100: Innovation language ("created," "designed," "invented"), creative industries, patent/publication, new program development
- 60-79: Some creative output, process innovation
- 40-59: Standard execution of existing processes
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 70 from resume alone

**Resilience (partial, 0-100)**
- Can infer from: career gaps followed by recovery, industry disruption navigation, startup experience
- 80-100: Major career recovery, thrived through disruption, startup survivor
- 60-79: Navigated some transitions successfully
- 40-59: Stable, no evidence of adversity navigation
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 65 from resume alone

**Adaptability (partial, 0-100)**
- Can infer from: industry changes, role pivots, geographic moves, diverse experiences
- 80-100: Multiple industry transitions, radical role changes, international experience
- 60-79: Some career pivots, demonstrated flexibility
- 40-59: Single-track career
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 65 from resume alone

**Trust (partial, 0-100)**
- Can infer from: promotions (employer trusted them with more), client retention, long tenures
- 80-100: Rapid promotions, trusted with increasing scope, long client relationships
- 60-79: Standard promotions, good tenure
- 40-59: Adequate but no strong trust signals
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 70 from resume alone

**Persuasion (partial, 0-100)**
- Can infer from: sales roles, fundraising, business development, stakeholder management
- 80-100: Revenue responsibility, fundraising success, deal closing, advocacy roles
- 60-79: Some sales/BD experience, stakeholder management
- 40-59: No direct persuasion evidence
- Cannot fully score without conversation; cap at 65 from resume alone

### NOT Scorable from Resume (must come from conversation)
- Emotional Regulation
- Empathy
- Confidence
- Tone (spoken)
- Nonverbal Communication
- Aesthetic Appreciation
- Perspective Taking (partially inferable but unreliable)
- Assertiveness (partially inferable but unreliable)
- Delivery (spoken; written delivery is Language Use)
- Topic Development (spoken)

## CONVERSATIONAL SCORING RUBRIC

When scoring from conversational responses, use these indicators:

### Cluster 1: Communication & Expression

**Verbal Communication**
- 80-100: Clear, articulate, well-structured responses; explains complex ideas simply; varies sentence structure; engages listener
- 60-79: Generally clear; occasional rambling; gets point across
- 40-59: Functional but choppy; relies on simple structures; sometimes unclear
- 20-39: Frequently unclear; disorganized thought expression

**Nonverbal Communication** (in text chat, infer from)
- Awareness of tone in writing; use of emphasis; ability to convey emotion through text
- Discussion of body language, presentations, or interpersonal awareness in scenarios
- 60 = default if no evidence either way (this is the hardest to assess in text)

**Language Use**
- Score directly from their writing quality in chat
- Same rubric as resume extraction but applied to conversational text

**Delivery**
- How well-structured are their scenario responses? Do they have a beginning, middle, end?
- 80-100: Clear narrative arc, concise, compelling
- 60-79: Gets there but meanders
- 40-59: Disorganized, hard to follow

**Tone**
- Do they calibrate appropriately to the conversation? Are they too casual when discussing professional scenarios, or too stiff?
- 80-100: Perfect calibration; shifts register fluidly
- 60-79: Generally appropriate
- 40-59: Somewhat miscalibrated

**Topic Development**
- When asked to describe a scenario, do they build the story? Add context? Develop the idea?
- 80-100: Rich, layered responses with context, nuance, and development
- 60-79: Adequate development, some depth
- 40-59: Surface-level, minimal development

**Formality**
- Similar to Tone but specifically about professional register
- Assessed from overall conversational style + scenario responses

### Cluster 2: Interpersonal & Collaboration

**Collaboration**
- In scenarios involving teams, do they default to: doing it themselves (low), delegating (medium), genuinely working with others and valuing input (high)?
- 80-100: Naturally references others' contributions, describes true collaborative process, values diverse input
- 60-79: Works with others but tends toward individual contribution
- 40-59: Primarily solo operator; team references feel performative

**Empathy**
- In scenarios involving conflict or others' struggles, do they demonstrate understanding of the other person's perspective and feelings?
- 80-100: Spontaneously considers emotional impact on others; mentions feelings; shows genuine concern
- 60-79: Acknowledges others' perspectives when prompted
- 40-59: Primarily self-focused in conflict scenarios

**Perspective Taking**
- Can they articulate WHY someone else might see things differently?
- 80-100: Proactively considers multiple viewpoints; understands systemic/contextual factors
- 60-79: Can see other perspectives when prompted
- 40-59: Struggles to move beyond own viewpoint

**Trust**
- Do their stories demonstrate reliability? Do they follow through? Do they reference being trusted by others?
- 80-100: Stories of being trusted with important things; consistent follow-through; others rely on them
- 60-79: Generally reliable signals
- 40-59: No strong trust signals

**Assertiveness**
- In conflict/disagreement scenarios, do they advocate for their position while remaining respectful?
- 80-100: Directly addresses disagreement; advocates clearly; doesn't avoid conflict; constructive
- 60-79: Will push back but somewhat reluctantly
- 40-59: Avoids conflict; defers to authority; doesn't advocate

### Cluster 3: Drive & Self-Management

**Initiative**
- Do their stories show them acting without being told, or waiting for direction?
- 80-100: Multiple examples of self-starting; sees problems and acts; creates opportunities
- 60-79: Acts proactively in familiar domains
- 40-59: Primarily responsive, not proactive

**Self-discipline**
- How do they describe managing long-term projects, goals, or habits?
- 80-100: Clear systems for self-management; sustained effort over time; delayed gratification
- 60-79: Generally disciplined; occasional struggles with follow-through
- 40-59: Relies heavily on external structure/deadlines

**Responsibility**
- Do they own outcomes in their stories, or externalize?
- 80-100: Clear ownership language; accountable for failures; doesn't blame
- 60-79: Generally accountable
- 40-59: Tends to externalize; attributes outcomes to circumstances

**Resilience**
- How do they describe navigating setbacks?
- 80-100: Concrete recovery stories; frames setbacks as learning; maintained forward momentum
- 60-79: Recovered but with difficulty; learned eventually
- 40-59: Setbacks were derailing; avoidance patterns

**Organization**
- How do they describe managing competing priorities?
- 80-100: Clear systems; prioritization frameworks; manages complexity well
- 60-79: Generally organized; some systems
- 40-59: Reactive; no clear prioritization method

### Cluster 4: Adaptability & Growth

**Adaptability**
- How do they respond to the "everything changes" scenario?
- 80-100: Energized by change; describes concrete adaptation strategies; comfortable with ambiguity
- 60-79: Adapts but prefers stability; manages change when forced
- 40-59: Resistant to change; stressed by ambiguity

**Curiosity**
- Do they ask YOU questions? Do they go deeper in their answers? Do they reference learning for its own sake?
- 80-100: Asks questions back; references learning; explores tangents; wide-ranging interests
- 60-79: Shows interest in learning when directly relevant
- 40-59: Purely practical; no exploration drive visible

**Creativity**
- In problem-solving scenarios, do they go beyond the obvious solution?
- 80-100: Novel approaches; unexpected connections; reframes problems; "what if" thinking
- 60-79: Some creative elements; generally conventional but with occasional sparks
- 40-59: Purely conventional; follows established patterns

**Aesthetic Appreciation**
- Do they reference quality, craft, design, or beauty in any context?
- 80-100: Spontaneously references aesthetics; values craft; notices quality
- 60-79: Some appreciation when prompted
- 40-59: No aesthetic dimension visible
- Note: This is the least critical subskill for most career matching. Default to 55 if no evidence.

### Cluster 5: Emotional & Professional Maturity

**Emotional Regulation**
- In the "public disagreement" or conflict scenarios, do they describe staying composed?
- 80-100: Describes pausing, breathing, choosing response; separates emotion from action; stays productive
- 60-79: Manages most situations but acknowledges occasional struggles
- 40-59: Reactive; emotions drive behavior in conflict

**Confidence**
- Do they describe their abilities with appropriate self-assurance? Not arrogant, not self-deprecating?
- 80-100: Owns strengths without bragging; comfortable with what they don't know; clear self-awareness
- 60-79: Generally confident; occasional hedging or underselling
- 40-59: Significant hedging; minimizes accomplishments; or conversely, overcompensates

**Persuasion**
- Can they make a case? In their stories, do they describe convincing others?
- 80-100: Clear examples of changing minds; structured arguments; uses evidence and emotion appropriately
- 60-79: Some persuasion ability; relies more on authority/position than skill
- 40-59: Doesn't attempt persuasion; accepts others' positions or avoids disagreement
