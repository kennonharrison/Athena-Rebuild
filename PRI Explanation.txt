## 1. What a PRI Is (Technical Definition)

A **Program-Related Investment (PRI)** is a capital deployment by a **private foundation** that:

1. Has as its **primary purpose** the accomplishment of a charitable mission (IRC §501(c)(3) purposes), and
2. Does **not** have income production or asset appreciation as a significant purpose, and
3. Does **not** support lobbying or political activity.

PRIs can take the form of:

* Low-interest loans
* Recoverable grants
* Loan guarantees
* Equity-like investments with capped returns

Unlike traditional grants, **PRIs are recyclable capital**: when repaid, the foundation can redeploy the funds toward new charitable activities.

## 4. PRI Instruments That Fit Norwood Creek Best

### 1. Low-Interest or Below-Market Loans

* Interest: 0–2%
* Term: 7–15 years
* Use: land improvements, regenerative infrastructure, workforce facilities

**Why this matters:** It lowers the farm’s weighted average cost of capital and reduces pressure to over-extract from land or labor.

---

### 2. Recoverable Grants

* Structured as a grant unless defined performance or revenue thresholds are met
* Repayment tied to cash flow, not fixed schedules

**Ideal for:** pilot programs (e.g., Skills2Work agricultural workforce pilots, mushroom or livestock rotations).

---

### 3. Loan Guarantees

* Foundation guarantees a portion of commercial debt
* Reduces lender risk and interest rates

**Strategic use:** unlocks bank financing for historic structures or processing facilities without the foundation directly lending all capital.

---

### 4. Mission-Capped Equity (Advanced but Powerful)

* Foundation receives a capped return (e.g., 1–3%)
* Excess value remains with the mission entity

**This reinforces your stated goal** that Norwood Creek is a **permanent, value-producing asset**, not an extractive or exit-driven vehicle.

## 7. Challenges and Constraints of Using PRIs (Important)

While powerful, PRIs introduce real constraints:

1. **Slower capital timelines**

   * Foundations move deliberately
   * Due diligence can take 6–18 months

2. **Restricted use of funds**

   * Capital must remain tightly linked to charitable outcomes
   * Limits flexibility compared to pure equity

3. **Governance and reporting burden**

   * Ongoing impact reporting is non-negotiable
   * Requires strong administrative discipline

4. **Foundation risk tolerance varies widely**

   * Some foundations are conservative despite PRI authority
   * Others require co-investment to proceed

## 8. Strategic Alternatives and Complements to PRIs

PRIs should not stand alone. Strong complements include:

* **NRCS, USDA, and state conservation cost-share programs**
* **Historic tax credits** paired with PRI bridge loans
* **Green banks or CDFIs** for blended capital stacks
* **Donor-advised fund recoverable grants** (similar economics, less bureaucracy)
* **Mission-aligned family offices** seeking preservation over yield

A **layered capital stack**—with PRIs absorbing early risk and commercial debt layered later—is the most durable configuration.
